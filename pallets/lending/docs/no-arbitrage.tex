\begin{abstract}
Following $\mu$'s method: for each non-trivial action cycle that
\textbf{starts and ends holding only TAO}, write the terminal TAO as a
factor of the initial holding, sized as a fraction $h$ of pool TAO, as a
function of LP state $(y,x,w_1)$ and lending parameters. Then minimise /
maximise that factor over the parameter space and ask whether any region
has expected value $>1$ (attacker profit).

Increments 1--2 recorded $\mu$'s $F$, Cycle~A, and the failure of
EMA/capacity protections under weight-updating re-add.
\textbf{Increment 3c} records the clone drain; answers $\mu$'s
``abandon Balancer / just Uniswap~v2?'' (no: swaps were already
fixed-weight weighted CPMM; Uni~v2 is only $w=\tfrac12$; only lending
readd skips asymmetric-join reweight); compares designs
(a)--(e); reports a bounded cycle search; and states a recommendation
for Jacob (ship~(a); harden with~(b) if needed). Full closed-form
proof remains open.
\end{abstract}

\section{Method (from $\mu$)}
\label{sec:method}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item Enumerate every non-trivial finite sequence of stake/unstake swaps
        and lending calls that \textbf{begins and ends with a pure-TAO
        book} (no open position, no residual alpha inventory attributed
        to the cycle).
  \item Write terminal TAO $V'$ as
        \[
          V' = F_{\mathrm{cyc}}(h;\, y,x,w_1;\, \Theta)\, V,
        \]
        where $V$ is the initial TAO stake of the cycle, $h = V/y$ (or
        another natural scale against pool TAO $y$), and $\Theta$ collects
        lending parameters (LTV, thin LTV, swap fee, EMA band, APR, term,
        capacity floors).
  \item Over a compact parameter domain (including SN29-like and deep
        equal-weight pools), compute
        $\min F_{\mathrm{cyc}}$ and $\max F_{\mathrm{cyc}}$.
  \item Positive EV for that cycle $\iff$ some feasible point has
        $F_{\mathrm{cyc}} > 1$ after fees (attacker convention below).
\end{enumerate}

\begin{remark}[Sign convention]
We take $F_{\mathrm{cyc}} > 1$ as \textbf{attacker profit} (ends with more
TAO than started, marking-to-market any returned alpha at the exit swap).
$\mu$'s September note used ``profitable when $F<1$'': that $F$ is a
\textbf{cost / survival factor for the pool (or for the round-trip
price)}, i.e.\ the reciprocal convention. We keep $\mu$'s symbol $F$ for
the September formula unchanged, and write $F_{\mathrm{cyc}}$ for
attacker-return factors. Relation when both apply to the same cycle:
$F_{\mathrm{cyc}} = 1/F$ up to fee placement.
\end{remark}

\section{$\mu$'s September $F$ (earlier proposal)}
\label{sec:mu-F}

Transcription checked against the hand-derived image:

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:mu-F}
F
=
(f_h+1)
\left(
\frac{(1-\varphi)^{1/w_2}}
{(1-\varphi)^{1/w_2}+f_h-\varphi}
\right)^{E},
\end{equation}
with exponent
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:mu-E}
E
=
\frac{f_h+1}
{(f_h+1+\varphi)
\left(
1-\dfrac{1-(1-\varphi)^{1/w_2}}{f_h-\varphi+1}
\right)^{w_2/(1-w_2)}
}.
\end{equation}

\begin{conjecture}[$\mu$, earlier design]
Along the September shorting cycle, the pool (or round-trip) factor is
$F$ above, and the cycle is attacker-profitable precisely when $F<1$.
\end{conjecture}

\label{sec:mu-map}

The September proposal predated forced-escrow longs and pull-on-borrow
lifts. A working identification used in this note (to be stress-tested

\begin{center}

\midrule

      & candidate: $h = P/y$ after lift, or LTV-scaled

\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

If $f_h$ is the \textbf{principal as a fraction of post-lift (or pre-trade)

side (thin vs floor $\times$ split). For LTV-bound opens with collateral
$C=\alpha y$, $P=\lambda C$ gives $f_h=\lambda\alpha$.
\end{remark}

\section{Does the same cycle exist today?}
\label{sec:applies}

\begin{cycle}[September-style short round trip]
Open a forced short of principal $P$ alpha (lift, sell $P$ into the pool,
escrow $E_S$), then close by buying $P$ back (escrow path) or by
repaying $P$ alpha from wallet and taking $E_S$.
\end{cycle}

Under the \textbf{buggy} design (readd via \texttt{adjust\_protocol\_liquidity}):
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Sell-path / escrow close} reverses the forced sale then
        re-adds $(\tau_L,P)$. With an intervening pump the re-add still
        reweights at the skewed ratio (a15 drain).
  \item \textbf{Wallet-path close} returns $E_S+C$ for alpha repaid from
        outside, then re-adds $(\tau_L,P)$ with the same weight update
        --- a \textbf{weight-ratchet} $\mu$'s fee-only $F$ misses
        (see Section~\ref{sec:gap}, Section~\ref{sec:clone}).
\end{itemize}

Under the \textbf{fixed} design (Proposition~\ref{prop:inverse}), both
closes credit reserves without touching $w_1$/$w_2$, so intervening swaps
cannot crystallize into $L$.

\section{TAO-pure cycles under the current design}
\label{sec:cycles}

Notation: attacker starts with TAO budget $V=h\,y$ (and, where needed,
an alpha inventory acquired inside the cycle by a swap that is part of
the cycle accounting).

\subsection{Cycle A --- long wallet ratchet (primary drain candidate)}
\label{cyc:A}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item (Optional) Sell alpha inventory into the pool (dump), moving spot
        down while the lending EMA lags.
  \item Open long: post collateral $C$ alpha, lift $g=P/y$, forced-buy
        escrow $A$ with $P$ TAO, subject to LTV, capacity, EMA band
        $p'\le \mathrm{EMA}\,(1+\beta)$.
  \item Close with $\mathtt{repay\_from\_wallet}=\mathsf{true}$: pay $P$
        TAO, receive $A+C$, re-add $(P,\alpha_L)$.
  \item Sell returned escrow $A$ for TAO.
\end{enumerate}
Mark-to-market: all alpha legs netted; terminal asset is TAO.
We write $F_A(h;y,x,w_1;\Theta)$ for $V'/V$.

\paragraph{Closed form (fee-free sketch, equal weights).}
Let $h=P/y\in(0,1)$, $w_1=w_2=\tfrac12$, ignore swap fees and EMA.
Lift removes $(P, hx)$ from $(y,x)$ in the sense $\alpha_L = h x$ when
$g=P/y=h$. Forced buy of $P$ on the lifted pool yields
\[
  A = (1-h)x\Bigl(1-\sqrt{\frac{1-h}{1-h+h}}\Bigr)
    = (1-h)x\Bigl(1-\sqrt{1-h}\Bigr).
\]
Post-open reserves $(y,\,x-\alpha_L-A)=(y,\,x(1-h)\sqrt{1-h})$.
Wallet repay + weight update $\to$ reserves $(y+P,\,x-A)$ with spot locked
at the post-open price; selling $A$ then gives
\[
  F_A^{\mathrm{update}}(h)
  =
  \frac{\Delta y_{\mathrm{sell}}(A;\,y+P,\,x-A,\,w')}{P},
\]
where $w'$ is the reweighted pair. With \emph{fixed} weights,
\[
  F_A^{\mathrm{keep}}(h)
  =
  \frac{y+P}{P}
  \left(
  1-\left(\frac{x-A}{x-A+A}\right)^{w_1/w_2}
  \right)
  =
  \frac{1+h}{h}
  \left(1-\left(1-\frac{A}{x}\right)\right)
  \quad\text{(for $w_1=w_2$)}
\]
reduces to a fee-free identity $F_A^{\mathrm{keep}}<1$ once the forced-buy
$A$ is substituted (exact algebra in increment~2). Numerics already show
the sign split (Section~\ref{sec:numeric}).

\subsection{Cycle B --- short wallet mirror}
Open short to the EMA band floor, wallet-repay with owned alpha, rebuy
alpha (or equivalently value the free $E_S$ against replacing $P$).
Factor $F_B$.

\subsection{Cycle C --- raise then wallet-close}
$\mathtt{open\_long\_and\_sell}$ with $S=A$ (near price-neutral by
design), then wallet-close the long and sell residual. Factor $F_C$.

\subsection{Cycle D --- escrow closes only}
Open long or short, close on the escrow AMM path (no wallet repay).
Fee-lossy by construction if re-add is the inverse of lift+trade.
Factors $F_D^{\mathrm{L}}, F_D^{\mathrm{S}}$.

\subsection{Cycle E --- partial repay interleaved with swaps}
Slice $k\in(0,1]$, swap, repay remainder. Factor $F_E$.

\section{Where a fee-only proof fails}
\label{sec:gap}

\begin{proposition}[Weight update on asymmetric re-add]
After a forced long buy the pool is at $(y,\,x-\alpha_L-A)$ with
\textbf{unchanged} weights and elevated spot. Wallet repay credits
$(P,\alpha_L)$ yielding reserves $(y+P,\,x-A)$. Calling
$\mathtt{update\_weights\_for\_added\_liquidity}$ sets new weights so
that the \emph{elevated} spot is preserved. Then $L=x^{w_1}y^{w_2}$
(and $w_1$) move monotonically with repeated cycles --- matching the
observed ``$w_1$ and $\sqrt{k}$ taking off''.
\end{proposition}

Consequently $\mu$'s $F$ (fees only, fixed weights) is a
\textbf{lower bound on pool protection} only for cycles that never hit
the weight-updating wallet re-add. For Cycle~A with weight update,
\[
  F_A \;\;\text{can exceed } 1
\]
even when the September $F$ satisfies $F\ge 1$.

\begin{remark}[Fix direction, not yet the proof]
Re-adding wallet-repay legs \emph{without} updating weights restores
the market-buy residual $(+P,-A)$ under fixed $(w_1,w_2)$. Numerics
(Section~\ref{sec:numeric}) show that change flips Cycle~A from $F_A>1$ to
$F_A<1$ on SN29-like pools. A full proof that $F_A\le 1$ under
preserve-weights is deferred to a later increment.
\end{remark}

\section{Theorem status (increment 1)}
\label{sec:status}

\begin{conjecture}[No TAO-pure arb, preserve-weights wallet re-add]
\label{conj:main}
If wallet-repay re-adds lift legs without updating Balancer weights,
and escrow closes re-add after reversing the forced trade, then for every
TAO-pure cycle of length $\le N$ (fees on, EMA fixed or free within
band), $F_{\mathrm{cyc}} \le 1$, with equality only in the zero-fee,
zero-size limit.
\end{conjecture}

\begin{theorem}[Status]
\label{thm:status}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Under \textbf{buggy} re-add (weight-updating via
        \texttt{adjust\_protocol\_liquidity}),
        Conjecture~\ref{conj:main} is \textbf{false}: Cycle~A and the
        clone short$\to$pump$\to$close sequences (Section~\ref{sec:clone}) are
        counterexamples with $F_{\mathrm{cyc}}>1$ and durable $w_1$/$L$
        corruption.
  \item $\mu$'s fee-only $F\ge 1$ does \textbf{not} imply
        $F_{\mathrm{cyc}}\le 1$ on that path, because $F$ omits the
        weight ratchet.
  \item Under design~(a) (Proposition~\ref{prop:inverse} /
        Theorem~\ref{thm:search}): bounded cycle search finds no
        $F_{\mathrm{cyc}}>1$; clone regressions assert no attacker
        profit and unchanged $w_1$. Full closed-form proof of
        Conjecture~\ref{conj:main} remains \textbf{open}.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\section{Do existing protections keep $EV\le 0$?}
\label{sec:protections}

Reachable $h$ on a \textbf{single} open (equal weights):
\begin{itemize}
  \item EMA band $+15\%$: fee-free buy move
        $p'/p=(1+h)^{1/w_1}=(1+h)^2\le 1.15$ $\Rightarrow$
        $h\le \sqrt{1.15}-1 \approx 0.0724$.
  \item Side capacity: $\approx 0.075$ (thin) to $\approx 0.165$
        (floor $\times$ split).
  \item $\mathtt{PrincipalExceedsTaoIn}$ / $\mathtt{AlphaIn}$:
        $P<y$ (and symmetrically for shorts) --- far looser than the
        band/capacity.
\end{itemize}

At the EMA cap ($h\approx 0.072$) with weight-update Cycle~A:
$F_A-1 \approx {+}0.43\%$ --- \textbf{still positive EV}. At capacity
$h=0.165$ (only reachable if spot was depressed or the EMA lagged):
$\approx{+}2.7\%$. Preserve-weights is negative at both points.

\paragraph{Splits and multiple coldkeys.}
Same-side opens are gated by the \emph{sticky} EMA: after one long, spot
is up and further longs hit the band sooner. That slows naive splitting,
but does not zero EV:
\begin{itemize}
  \item $N$ sequential opens at $h=0.05$ (inside the band) each earn
        $\approx{+}0.15\%$ while $w_1$ ratchets ($0.500\to 0.513$ by
        $N=10$).
  \item A pre-dump that pushes spot below the EMA, then one long up to
        $\mathrm{EMA}\cdot 1.15$, realises a \emph{much} larger effective
        $h$ and the $10^2$--$10^3\,\tau$ profits of Section~\ref{sec:numeric}.
  \item Multiple coldkeys in one block still serialize; they share the
        same post-trade spot/EMA constraint, but can alternate sides or
        wait for EMA catch-up.
\end{itemize}

\begin{theorem}[Protections vs Cycle~A]
\label{thm:prot}
EMA band, side capacity, and principal-vs-reserve checks \textbf{bound}
$h$ but \textbf{do not} imply $F_A\le 1$ on the weight-updating wallet
path. Inside the reachable set, $F_A>1$ for all $h\gtrsim 0.031$. Only
weight-preserving wallet re-add flips the sign through the whole
sweep.
\end{theorem}

\noindent On profitable closes, $L\approx x^{1-w_1}y^{w_1}$ jumps with $w_1$
(a13: $174\mathrm{k}\to 203\mathrm{k}$) and \textbf{does not revert} after
unstake. AlphaIn mean-reverts; the durable corruption is $w_1$/$L$, which
lets the exit unstake extract more TAO than the pump deposited.

\paragraph{Mechanism.}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \texttt{lift\_protocol\_liquidity} removes $(g\,y,g\,x)$
        \textbf{without} updating weights (proportional remove).
  \item Intervening \texttt{AddStake}/swap skews the reserve ratio;
        weights stay put.
  \item Buggy \texttt{readd\_protocol\_liquidity} routed through
        \texttt{adjust\_protocol\_liquidity} $\to$
        \texttt{update\_weights\_for\_added\_liquidity} at the
        \textbf{post-pump} ratio, rewriting $w_1$/$w_2$ and inflating
        $L$.
  \item Subsequent \texttt{RemoveStake} against corrupted weights is
        extractive.
\end{enumerate}
This is $\mu$'s open$\to$swap$\to$close framing with $F_{\mathrm{cyc}}>1$
driven by the weight ratchet, not by fee-only $F<1$.

\section{Answer to mu: we do not abandon Balancer}
\label{sec:balancer-coherence}

\begin{remark}[Scope of the bypass]
Ordinary \textbf{stake / unstake} (and every other AMM swap) still execute
on the Balancer weighted pool with the stored $(w_1,w_2)$:
\[
  p=\frac{w_1}{w_2}\frac{y}{x},\qquad
  L=x^{w_1}y^{w_2}.
\]
Emissions still call \texttt{adjust\_protocol\_liquidity}, which may
\texttt{update\_weights\_for\_added\_liquidity} so a one-sided injection
keeps spot. \textbf{Only} lending's \texttt{lift}/\texttt{readd} pair
bypasses that weight update. The bypass is not ``leave Balancer'' --- it is
``use the proportional remove/add inverse, not the
asymmetric-join reweight.''
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[Coherence while a position is open]
\label{prop:open-coherent}
After a lift of fraction $g$, the live pool is
$((1-g)y,(1-g)x,w_1,w_2)$. Spot is unchanged, $L\mapsto(1-g)L$, and
every subsequent stake/unstake is a standard Balancer swap on that
thinner book. Custody holds the removed legs; they are not part of the
AMM state. So pricing during the open interval is exactly Balancer.
\end{proposition}

\section{Two readd designs}
\label{sec:designs}

Lift always snapshots the removed legs $(\Delta y,\Delta x)=(gy,gx)$ and
the invariant slice $\Delta L = g\,L$. After intervening swaps the live
ratio may differ. Two ways to close the Balancer side:

\paragraph{(a) Snapshot / restore exact reserve deltas (weights untouched).}
Credit exactly $(\Delta y,\Delta x)$ into current reserves; leave
$(w_1,w_2)$ alone.
\begin{itemize}
  \item No intervening swaps: exact inverse --- $p$ and $L$ restored.
  \item With intervening swaps: $w$ unchanged; $L$ after credit is
        \emph{not} generally $L_0$ (homogeneous add of a stale-ratio
        vector into a skewed book). Spot may jump on the credit.
  \item This is an unbalanced join \emph{without} the Balancer
        price-preserving weight rewrite (and without an extra join fee).
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{(b) Settle in invariant units (L-conserving proportional join).}
Treat the position as owning $\Delta L$ LP shares. On readd, join
proportionally at the \emph{current} ratio,
$(sy_{\mathrm{skew}},sx_{\mathrm{skew}})$ with $s=\Delta L/L_{\mathrm{skew}}$,
so spot is preserved and $L$ rises by $\Delta L$. Custody holds
$(\Delta y,\Delta x)\ne(sy,sx)$; the residual must be mark-to-market
(one Balancer swap). That residual swap is a new surface.

\begin{proposition}[Buggy readd is neither (a) nor (b)]
\label{prop:buggy}
\texttt{adjust\_protocol\_liquidity}$\to$\texttt{update\_weights\_for\_added\_liquidity}
credits $(\Delta y,\Delta x)$ \emph{and} solves for new weights that keep
post-credit spot equal to the skewed mark. That is the asymmetric-join
tool for emissions, not the inverse of a proportional lift. Empirically
it inflates $L$ and unlocks extractive unstake (a13/a15).
\end{proposition}

\section{Root fix = design (a)}
\label{sec:inverse}

\begin{proposition}[Design (a) is the Balancer inverse of lift]
\label{prop:inverse}
Lift of fraction $g$ yields
$((1-g)y,(1-g)x,w_1,w_2)$. Crediting the same legs $(\Delta y,\Delta x)$
\textbf{without} changing weights is the set-theoretic inverse on the
no-swap path. With intervening swaps, (a) still leaves $(w_1,w_2)$
invariant (the property stake/unstake need); it does \emph{not} claim
$L=L_0$ after credit.
\end{proposition}

\begin{corollary}[Implementation]
\label{cor:impl}
\texttt{readd\_protocol\_liquidity} $=$ pure reserve credit (no weight
write, no reservoir). Emissions keep calling
\texttt{adjust\_protocol\_liquidity}. Lending
\texttt{readd\_and\_credit} / \texttt{\_preserve\_weights} both use (a).
\end{corollary}

\section{Follow-up: ``is this just Uniswap v2?''}
\label{sec:univ2}

$\mu$: \emph{if you don't change (or re-derive) the weights, it's
basically back to Uniswap~v2.}

\subsection{What main actually re-derives}
Main's Balancer has \textbf{two} distinct operations:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Swaps} (stake / unstake): reserves move on the curve
        $L=x^{w_1}y^{w_2}$ at the \emph{stored} weights. Weights are
        \textbf{not} re-derived mid-swap. This was already true before
        lending existed.
  \item \textbf{Asymmetric liquidity adds} (emissions via
        \texttt{adjust\_protocol\_liquidity}): reserves receive an
        arbitrary $(\mathrm{d}y,\mathrm{d}x)$; then
        \texttt{update\_weights\_for\_added\_liquidity} solves for new
        $(w_1,w_2)$ so that post-add spot equals pre-add spot. That is
        the price-neutral join. It is \emph{not} part of the swap path.
\end{enumerate}
So ``Balancer behaviour'' on main $=$ fixed-weight weighted CPMM for
trades, plus optional weight re-derivation on protocol/user
\emph{liquidity adds} so those adds do not move price.

\subsection{Fixed weights $\neq$ Uniswap v2}
Uniswap~v2 is the \textbf{special case} $w_1=w_2=\tfrac12$ of the
Balancer invariant ($L=\sqrt{xy}$, $p=y/x$). With $w_1\neq w_2$ held
fixed, the pool is still a \textbf{weighted} Balancer CPMM:
\[
  p=\frac{w_1}{w_2}\frac{y}{x},\qquad
  L=x^{w_1}y^{w_2},
\]
with the same swap closed forms main already uses. SN29-like books run
near $w_1\approx 0.47$, not $\tfrac12$. Design~(a) does not force
equal weights; it refuses to \emph{rewrite} them on lending readd.

\subsection{What is true while a lift is outstanding}
After lift, $(w_1,w_2)$ are unchanged (lift is proportional). Every
swap against the thinner book is therefore a fixed-weight Balancer
swap --- \textbf{exactly as every swap already was}. Lending did not
``switch the AMM off Balancer''; swaps never consulted the asymmetric-join
reweight. The only behavioural change under (a) is on the
\textbf{readd after skew}: instead of a price-neutral asymmetric join
(reweight to keep the post-pump spot), the protocol credits the
snapshot legs at fixed $w$, which can move spot and need not restore
$L_0$.

\subsection{What is lost versus ``full'' Balancer join behaviour?}
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lp{0.62\textwidth}@{}}
\toprule
Surface & Under design~(a) \\
\midrule
Stake / unstake & Unchanged: weighted CPMM at stored $w$. \\
Emissions / protocol adds & Unchanged: still
  \texttt{adjust\_protocol\_liquidity} + weight re-derivation. \\
User LP-style asymmetric adds & Unchanged (lending is not that path). \\
Lending readd after skew & \textbf{Lost:} price-neutrality of the
  asymmetric join. Readd may move spot; $L$ may drift from $L_0$. \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\noindent\textbf{Is that acceptable?} Yes, as an emergency root fix:
readd is the protocol restoring its own previously removed inventory,
not a third-party LP joining at the mark. The property we must not
give attackers is ``crystallize a pumped spot into $w$ and inflate
$L$.'' Giving up price-neutral readd for that one path is the cost.
Bounded search (Section~\ref{sec:search}): no profitable cycle on the
grid. Full proof still open.

\section{Designs that keep re-derivation elsewhere}
\label{sec:keep-rederive}

Goal: emissions / other asymmetric adds still re-derive weights;
lending's open$\to$swap$\to$close cycle is non-extractable.

\paragraph{(a) --- shipped.}
Lending: fixed-$w$ credit of snapshot deltas. Elsewhere: re-derive as
today. Pros: smallest diff; exact lift inverse on the quiet path;
kills a13/a15; search-clean. Cons: readd can move spot; $L$ not
restored after skew; proof open.

\paragraph{(b) --- $\Delta L$ proportional join + residual MTM.}
Lending readd never needs weight re-derivation (join is proportional at
the \emph{current} ratio). Residual custody vs proportional basket is
one Balancer swap (fees apply). Pros: spot preserved on join; $L$
restored by construction; search-clean; closest to ``Balancer LP
semantics.'' Cons: new residual-swap accounting; residual is a surface
to audit; more code.

\paragraph{(c) --- re-derive against the \emph{lift-time} ratio.}
Idea: call \texttt{update\_weights\_for\_added\_liquidity} with the
lift-time book (or the lift ratio) instead of the skewed mark.
But the lift legs $(\Delta y,\Delta x)$ are \emph{proportional} to the
lift-time reserves, so a weight update computed against that ratio is
a \textbf{no-op} --- same $w$ as (a). If instead one reweights the
\emph{skewed} post-credit reserves so that spot equals the
\textbf{lift-time} spot, that forces price back to a historical level
and is a different (likely more dangerous) policy. Verdict: ``re-derive
at lift-time ratio'' for proportional lift legs collapses to (a); the
force-price-to-lift-spot reading is not recommended.

\paragraph{(d) --- charge / target the EMA ratio on readd.}
Variant: proportional join aimed at the lending EMA price, or reweight
so post-readd spot equals EMA. Pros: ties exit to the same oracle the
band/settlement already use. Cons: EMA can lag (the band exists
because of that); targeting EMA on readd can itself be gamed by
pre-moving spot then waiting; does not by itself explain a13 (drain was
weight crystallisation at the \emph{current} mark, not EMA). Useful as
a settlement policy later; not the minimal drain fix.

\paragraph{(e) --- keep current-mark reweight but haircut / fee the
join.}
Still rewrites $w$ toward the pumped mark; a fee may shrink but not
remove the ratchet under repetition. Rejected as the primary fix.

\section{Recommendation for Jacob}
\label{sec:jacob}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Ship design~(a) now} (already in the swap pallet):
        lending lift/readd keep weights fixed; emissions keep
        re-deriving. This does \emph{not} reduce the AMM to Uniswap~v2
        unless weights were already $\tfrac12$; swaps remain weighted
        Balancer. What is lost is only price-neutral lending readd after
        skew --- acceptable against the reproduced drain.
  \item \textbf{Do not} restore current-mark
        \texttt{update\_weights\_for\_added\_liquidity} on lending
        readd. That is the bug.
  \item \textbf{Next hardening (optional, preferred if (a) ever shows a
        counterexample):} design~(b) --- restore $\Delta L$ by a
        current-ratio proportional join and mark-to-market the custody
        residual with an explicit Balancer swap (fee on). Keeps
        re-derivation for emissions; gives price-neutral lending readd
        without crystallising the pump into $w$.
  \item Treat (c) ``lift-time re-derive'' as equivalent to (a) for
        proportional legs; do not chase (d)/(e) as the root fix.
  \item Status: bounded cycle search clears (a) and (b); closed-form
        no-arb proof remains open. Skeptic/Auditor should treat (a) as
        ``search-backed emergency inverse,'' not ``theorem-closed.''
\end{enumerate}
