---
title: Pool reserves and native lending
description: How the proposed migration preserves opening price and local sensitivity, creates separate lending reserves and supports governance price-impact tuning and fixed principal subnet loans.
---

This upgrade moves subnet pools to a translated ellipse while preserving each
pool's opening price and local price sensitivity. Balances outside the new
curve's trading range fund a separate lending vault. Borrowers can then
receive freely usable alpha against TAO collateral or borrow transferable TAO
against alpha collateral.

This page describes the proposed implementation. It is not a mainnet launch
announcement. Borrowing is enabled only after the complete pool migration
succeeds. If migration cannot complete, it stays disabled. Governance can pause
new loans; repayment remains available independently of that safety switch.

## Pool migration

Each dynamic subnet pool moves from weighted Balancer pricing to a translated
ellipse, a superellipse with exponent two. Its scales and centers preserve the
pool's opening price and local price sensitivity. The curve parameters stay
fixed after swaps, and later liquidity injections preserve the existing curve
coordinates instead of recalibrating its depth.

Migration does not automatically impose a minimum price movement. Each subnet's
price-impact target starts disabled. Preserving local sensitivity means the
price response of a very small trade is unchanged at migration; finite trades
differ because the ellipse has a different shape. Purchases and sales need not
have exactly equal percentage impact. Governance can enable the tuning mechanism
described below; a network-wide 1% target remains deferred.

The finite buy and sell boundaries remain. An order must fit the available
curve range, output reserves and caller price limits. Empty pools and root are
not funded by this migration. Lending inventory comes from the baseline
ellipse's unreachable floors, without additional tightening of the curve.

## Governance price-impact dial

Root governance can call `Lending.set_min_price_impact(netuid, bps)` to set a
subnet's minimum ending spot-price movement for a fixed **500-TAO reference
trade**. The target applies in both directions: a purchase adding 500 net TAO
to the curve, and a sale whose net alpha input is worth 500 TAO at the price
before that trade. Traders pay swap fees in addition to these net reference
inputs. A value of `100` means 100 basis points, or 1%. Nonzero targets range
from 1 to 5,000 basis points. The reference size is fixed in V1; this call changes
the percentage.

This measures mechanical price impact, not realised market volatility or the
trader's average execution slippage. Fees and average execution prices are
separate. The geometric target applies when the complete reference trade fits the
remaining curve range and reserves; it does not make an otherwise blocked trade
executable or override caller price limits.

Tightening contracts the ellipse's remaining range around its current trading
point while keeping the opening spot price unchanged within fixed-point rounding.
It uses the curve's least-sensitive regions to establish a conservative geometric
minimum for later complete reference trades on either side. This minimum describes
the continuous curve; future executable quotes also include atomic token and
fixed-point rounding. Consequently, the current impact can
be greater than the selected minimum. The parameters remain fixed through
subsequent swaps: the chain does not recalibrate after every trade. Activation
requires both full reference trades to fit; a target that would prevent either
one fails atomically.

The call transfers newly unreachable alpha and TAO floors to the existing
lending vault, preserving separate active reserves and real lending inventory.
It requires a ready, funded vault and is unavailable during subnet dissolution.
Outstanding principal, coupons, borrower collateral and the 24-hour lending
reference remain unchanged. Tighter curves reduce remaining buy and sell
capacity. A short's optional collateral-funded buyback can therefore become
infeasible; repayment of its fixed alpha debt from the saved hotkey remains
available.

Lowering the target, or setting it to `0`, relaxes or disables the policy. It
does not widen the curve or return previously extracted inventory to trading
reserves. A later increase only tightens when the existing curve is insufficient.
This governance call adds no borrower-facing command: the public lending CLI
still has only `open`, `close` and `list`.

## Where lending inventory comes from

An ellipse has finite trading endpoints. At the sell endpoint its marginal
alpha price reaches zero. At the buy endpoint its marginal price grows without
bound. A valid swap stays inside that positive-price range, respects the
remaining capacity, and never executes at zero or infinite price.

Consequently, some physical alpha and TAO cannot be withdrawn through swaps
while these parameters stay fixed. The migration calculates these global
reserve floors and transfers them into the lending vault. It subtracts each
extracted amount from the corresponding ellipse center as well as the active
reserve, preserving the translated coordinates and the rebuilt swap quotes.
Alpha and TAO can be extracted independently; paired proportional liquidity
withdrawal is unnecessary. The transfer creates no new tokens.

The accounting separates active AMM reserves, available lending inventory,
outstanding principal, borrower collateral and collected
interest awaiting a TAO burn or alpha conversion. Extracted balances become
real lending assets; they are not also available to pay swaps.

## Open and close a short

A short locks TAO collateral and receives real alpha on its selected hotkey.
The borrower can sell, transfer, use or pledge that alpha elsewhere. Opening
does not sell it through the AMM or create locked sale proceeds. There is one
position per coldkey and subnet.

For collateral `C` and lending reference price `E`, alpha debt `D` is bounded by
both `D × E ≤ C / 4` and the alpha obtainable from a simulated purchase spending
`C / 4` TAO. The simulated purchase must fit the curve and includes swap fees;
it limits the loan without executing a swap. Available inventory and the
aggregate borrowing cap must also permit the loan. The caller protects
additional alpha principal and its opening TAO valuation separately. The legacy
call field `min_proceeds` now means minimum opening valuation, not sale proceeds.

Alpha borrowing also limits the debt's **conservative immediate funded
redemption value to 25% of remaining collateral**, including payout rounding.
This prevents a low market price from granting an alpha claim worth more than
the posted TAO at the current deregistration state. The calculation overestimates
the possible ordinary TAO pot and underestimates the guaranteed alpha claims
sharing it after the proposed alpha withdrawal.

The pot bound counts active and latent pool TAO plus unloaned vault TAO. For
legacy subnets whose ordinary payouts exclude pool alpha, the protected claim
lower bound counts only protocol alpha. For newer subnets it also counts active
and latent pool alpha and the unloaned vault alpha remaining after the proposed
withdrawal. Ordinary holder aggregates and pending fees do not enlarge this
protected lower bound. A required bound that cannot be established refuses the
quote and opening; a zero claim lower bound with a positive pot cannot admit
alpha borrowing. Thus a funded vault does not guarantee that either loan type
can currently open.

For positive claim lower bound `N`, pot upper bound `P` and quarter collateral
`Q`, all in base units, a sufficient combined alpha-debt bound is
`D ≤ floor(Q × N / (P + N))`. This covers
`ceil(D × P / N)` plus at most one TAO base unit of payout rounding for each
borrowed alpha base unit. A conservative snapshot uses the remaining vault
alpha after the initial candidate loan; clipping that candidate to a smaller
loan can only improve this backing. Each other existing alpha loan must also
remain fully covered by its own TAO collateral after rounded-up accrued
interest. One position's surplus cannot cover another's deficit.

For example, 1,000 TAO collateral can support an opening loan valued at up to
250 TAO, subject to the funded-claim limit and inventory. Suppose its fixed debt
is 200 alpha. That debt stays **200 alpha** unless
the position borrows more or repays; coupons do not amortize it.

At an ordinary close, the owner returns the total alpha principal from the
saved hotkey. The alpha need not be the originally borrowed units: buying it
later or obtaining it elsewhere repays the same fixed debt. The contract
returns that alpha to the lending vault and refunds the remaining TAO collateral.

The owner can instead request a buyback through at most six internal AMM orders
in one atomic operation. It can spend only remaining TAO collateral after
interest, with no assumed sale proceeds or external spending. If the complete
buyback cannot fit the curve or the collateral, it fails without changing the
position. Both paths collect interest through the closing block.

## Borrow TAO against alpha

A long locks existing free alpha stake from a selected hotkey. The contract
loans TAO from the separate vault directly to the coldkey's free balance. That
TAO may be transferred, used to buy alpha or used elsewhere. Assets purchased
with it are not assumed to remain in contract custody.

For alpha collateral `A`, the TAO debt cannot exceed `A × E / 4` or the net
TAO proceeds of a simulated sale of `A / 4`. This simulation does not sell the
locked collateral. The full quote must execute. A further funded-redemption
limit can reduce the loan below both amounts, and vault inventory and the
aggregate borrowing cap must also permit it.

The funded-redemption limit values the collateral using only TAO that would
still be available for ordinary deregistration payouts after the new loan.
It assumes all active AMM TAO could be withdrawn by subsequent alpha sales,
so only the unloaned TAO vault inventory counts as backing. Outstanding TAO
principal, pending interest and anticipated recoveries from other positions do
not count. A conservative count of potential alpha redemption claims determines
the collateral's share of that backing: pool alpha, protocol alpha, actual staked
alpha and the protocol alpha buffer restored before deregistration payouts.
Borrowing TAO waits until the actual-stake aggregate has finished backfilling.

The new TAO loan must be no more than **25% of this stressed funded value**.
The withdrawal must also leave every existing TAO loan fully covered by its
own remaining alpha collateral, after deducting interest accrued through the
current block. Surplus coverage on one loan cannot offset a deficit on another.
Quotes and loan openings apply the same checks across all borrowers on the
subnet.

For example, let `B` be the unloaned TAO vault inventory before opening,
`N` the conservative alpha claim count and `A` the new alpha collateral.
After lending `L` TAO, that collateral's stressed redemption is
`A × (B − L) / N`. Applying 25% LTV therefore limits the new loan to
`L ≤ A × B / (4 × N + A)`, rounded down, before the other opening checks.
This accounts for the loan's own withdrawal rather than valuing collateral
against backing that the borrower is about to remove.

Closing requires the owner to repay the total TAO principal from free
balance. The remaining alpha collateral is then returned to the saved hotkey.
The loan does not automatically reverse trades the borrower made elsewhere.

## Increase a position

Use the same `open` command again to add collateral and borrow more. The subnet,
side and hotkey must match the saved position. There remains one position per
coldkey and subnet: its collateral and debt grow together, and one close repays
the total principal. Additional borrowed assets are delivered freely too.

The chain collects interest accrued on the existing position before adding the
new collateral. The combined position must pass current opening limits, not
just the additional loan by itself. Alpha borrowing includes the 25% conservative
funded-claim limit and full coverage of other alpha loans. For TAO borrowing this
includes the 25%
stressed funded-redemption limit after the additional withdrawal, plus coverage
of other TAO loans. Growth remains subject to the 10% aggregate borrowing cap
and available inventory. An already exhausted position cannot be increased.

Each increase fixes a coupon for the additional borrowing at its current
opening valuation and adds it to the existing annual coupon. Earlier coupons
are not repriced, fractional interest is carried forward, and the weekly
collection schedule is not restarted. An increase uses the existing position
slot. Its quote and caller bounds refer to **additional principal, opening value
and annual interest**; position reads and `list` report the combined totals.
The `Increased` event likewise reports the added collateral, principal, proceeds
and annual coupon rather than the resulting totals. New shorts report zero
proceeds in that compatibility event field.

## Interest and forfeiture

The launch rate is **100% nominal annual interest on opening loan value**, fixed
in collateral units at opening. For shorts, annual TAO interest equals the
alpha principal's value at the opening lending EMA, rounded up. For longs, the TAO principal is
converted into alpha at the opening lending reference, rounded up; that fixes
annual alpha interest. Later market prices do not change the collateral coupon.
There is no additional fee for opening or closing a loan.

Interest accrues per block. Collection is scheduled weekly and processed within
the chain's available weight budget; increasing or closing also collects accrued
interest. A 250-TAO opening loan pays approximately
4.79 TAO per seven days on a 365-day year. With 1,000 TAO collateral and no earlier
close, that corresponds to approximately four years of coupons. It is not
100% interest on the entire 1,000-TAO collateral.

**Both short and long coupons burn TAO.** A short already pays interest in TAO:
the collected coupon moves to the vault's reserve account and then to the
canonical inaccessible TAO burn address (`BurnAccountId`). This needs no AMM
swap or mature price reference.

A long pays interest in collateral alpha. The chain sells that alpha through
the existing executable AMM path and burns exactly the TAO it receives. The
complete, fee-inclusive sale must return at least 98% of a mature lending EMA's
fair output, including fees and price impact. Bounded chunking can sell a
smaller acceptable amount; favorable prices are allowed. The sale and exact TAO
burn are atomic: if either fails, the original alpha remains pending in custody.
A failed short-coupon burn likewise retains the collected TAO for retry.

Collected interest does not replenish available loan inventory or enlarge the
borrowing cap. The canonical burn transfers TAO to the inaccessible burn
address; both the currency and Subtensor total-issuance counters remain
unchanged. It does not use TAO recycling, which reduces issuance.

A TAO coupon too small to initialize an empty destination account remains an
explicit dust exception: it is recycled and recorded as `DustForfeited`, rather
than credited or reported as an interest burn. This can apply to a tiny first
coupon when a vault holds only alpha.

There are no price-triggered liquidations. When collateral is exhausted, the
chain forfeits and finalizes the position, retains assets still held in custody,
and records unrecovered principal as a loss. Freely transferred borrowed assets
are not reclaimed or described as repayment when they were never returned.
The borrower cannot later revive a forfeited position to recover its assets.
This mechanism deliberately accepts credit losses; a high coupon and low LTV
do not guarantee principal recovery.

## Reference price and borrowing limits

Loans use a dedicated geometric price EMA with a 24-hour half-life at normal
12-second blocks. It is updated once per completed block, so the reference for
an opening is fixed before that block's trades. Each observation is clipped
between half and twice the previous reference before updating. Existing price
history is retained during the price-preserving migration; references without
valid history must warm up before borrowing.

The reference does not make manipulation mathematically impossible. The
executable-depth checks, collateral and borrowing caps provide additional limits.
The chain does not impose a blanket borrowing pause merely because spot and
the EMA disagree.

Both loan types additionally use conservative funded-redemption checks. Alpha
loans bound the maximum funded claim that borrowed alpha could carry; TAO loans
bound the minimum stressed payout of pledged alpha. A market price or slow EMA
alone is insufficient. New borrowing cannot consume the conservative coverage
required by other existing loans. These checks constrain openings and increases;
they do not guarantee future coverage as interest consumes collateral or pool
assets and alpha claims change.

At launch, outstanding borrowing in each asset may not exceed **10% of that
asset's funded vault inventory**: available inventory plus outstanding fixed
principal. This is an aggregate subnet cap, not a per-user allowance or a share
of circulating subnet supply. Collateral, AMM reserves and
pending interest do not enlarge it. Principal repayments replenish inventory in
the original borrowed asset; interest burns do not. Written-off principal
reduces the funded base.

V1 supports at most 256 funded subnet vaults, bounding reference-update work.
When those slots are full, additional pools retain their reserves in the AMM
and cannot open loans. Automatic admission checks at most one subnet per block
and resumes funding after a completed subnet deregistration frees a vault slot.
The baseline ellipse migration still applies to those deferred subnets.

V1 additionally limits open positions to 128 per subnet and 256 across the
chain. Closing releases position slots. A borrower's own coldkey and hotkey
changes are blocked until closing. If a validator moves a hotkey's nominated
stake, positions follow the actual stake migration so their repayment source and collateral destination
remain correct. Keeping stake on the old hotkey also keeps the position's source
there.

## Subnet deregistration

Deregistration stops new loans and freezes interest at a common cutoff. It
also freezes the dedicated 24-hour lending EMA and the existing 2-hour fast EMA.
Their higher value is the historical short-debt mark; if the fast EMA is absent,
the lending EMA supplies the mark. This replaces a live spot-price settlement.

Existing beta-basket holdings first convert to funded root cash through their
own AMM path. Holdings blocked by a curve endpoint retain alpha for ordinary
funded redemption into the originating validator's root cash slot. Pending TAO
coupons are burned and unloaned lending assets return before the ordinary
dissolution pot and eligible alpha claim count are fixed.

Ordinary alpha holders then receive the actual funded pro-rata redemption
rate. Freely held borrowed alpha is real alpha and receives the same treatment;
the chain does not invent a repayment or exclude it from ordinary claims.
Pledged alpha likewise has no priority over other holders.

After ordinary payouts complete, a short's fixed alpha debt is valued at the
**higher of its frozen historical mark and its funded redemption value**,
rounded conservatively. The chain takes at most that amount from the remaining
TAO collateral, recovers principal, returns any surplus and records a shortfall
when collateral cannot cover the debt. The funded valuation includes an
allowance for ordinary payout rounding. The borrower does not need to return
alpha during this terminal settlement; there is no AMM buyback.

Recovery cannot exceed the remaining TAO collateral. The opening 25% market
LTV does not guarantee coverage of a later funded redemption value, especially
after interest deductions. The terminal valuation prevents an artificially low
debt mark, but it cannot create missing funds; this design accepts that loss.

Pending alpha interest stays ordinary vault stake through global settlement.
The chain burns only the actual funded TAO redemption receipts for that
interest, without an AMM swap. Alpha with no funded TAO receipt produces no TAO
burn; a spot or EMA valuation is not a funded receipt.

Longs accumulate **actual funded TAO redemptions** across all of the position
escrow's alpha holdings, then settle once after every redemption is complete.
They do not promise to pay an EMA or spot valuation. A long
owing 50 TAO whose collateral redeems for 70 TAO repays 50 and refunds 20. If it
redeems for 20 TAO, the chain recovers 20 and records a 30-TAO loss. Freely
transferred TAO is not reclaimed from elsewhere. Recovery is routed separately
from the already-fixed payout pot to avoid counting the same funds twice.
Both short and long principal recoveries go to the existing global protocol
recovery destination, outside the already-fixed ordinary payout pot. They
cannot enlarge holders' payouts or be counted twice. The conservative opening
valuation does not create a different payout rate or replace actual funded
receipts. No lending position performs a terminal AMM swap.

Tiny terminal refunds below the account minimum cannot recreate a reaped
account. These amounts are recycled explicitly and recorded as `DustForfeited`;
the settlement event reports only the refund actually credited.

Position and custody records must finish cleanup before the subnet identifier
can be reused. Deregistration is terminal settlement, despite the absence of
price-triggered liquidations during normal operation.

## Three CLI commands

Install the SDK release shipped with the lending runtime. Use your intended
network and wallet; the examples below use subnet 64 as an example, not a
statement that its lending vault is enabled or has sufficient inventory.

```bash
# Prompt for collateral with an estimated minimum for a 1-TAO loan opening value.
btcli lending open --netuid 64 --side short --hotkey <hotkey> -w mywallet

# Lock 1,000 TAO and receive freely usable borrowed alpha on this hotkey.
btcli lending open --netuid 64 --side short --collateral 1000 -w mywallet

# Lock 700 free alpha from this hotkey and receive transferable TAO.
btcli lending open --netuid 64 --side long --collateral 700 --hotkey <hotkey> -w mywallet

# Add 200 more TAO collateral and increase the same short.
btcli lending open --netuid 64 --side short --collateral 200 -w mywallet

# Repay total principal from your wallet and release remaining collateral.
btcli lending close --netuid 64 -w mywallet

# A short can instead buy back its debt using only remaining TAO collateral.
btcli lending close --netuid 64 --no-repay-from-wallet -w mywallet

# List all positions on a subnet, or one owner's position.
btcli lending list --netuid 64
btcli lending list --netuid 64 --coldkey <coldkey>
```

When `--collateral` is omitted on an interactive terminal, the CLI resolves the
wallet and hotkey first, then estimates the minimum additional collateral for
a loan opening value of at least 1 TAO (or the runtime's minimum if higher).
The estimate is offered as the collateral prompt's default; press Enter to
accept it or enter another amount. For shorts, this is the TAO opening value
of borrowed alpha, not sale proceeds. For longs, it is the borrowed TAO amount.
Collateral is entered in TAO for shorts and subnet alpha for longs.

The estimate uses runtime quotes at one block, including the owner's existing
position, redemption coverage and borrowing limits. It is a quote of the
required collateral, not a check that the wallet holds that collateral.
The selected amount is quoted again before submission because chain state can
change. A warming reference or insufficient inventory can prevent an estimate;
the CLI reports the failure instead of offering an unverified amount. Scripts
must provide `--collateral` explicitly.

Open and close obtain complete quotes from the runtime. By default,
`--max-slippage 1` sets an opening principal floor and, for shorts, a separate
opening TAO valuation floor, or a closing payment ceiling and refund floor, with a 1%
margin. The entire call rolls back if its bounds fail.
The CLI quotes using the signing owner and selected hotkey, so an increase is
checked against the saved position. An unavailable quote stops submission
instead of silently dropping protection.
Quote refusals are decoded into named errors with explanations, including
`ReferenceWarmingUp`, `AmountTooSmall` and `InsufficientRedemptionBacking`.
`AmountTooSmall` can mean that the calculated loan opening value is below the
minimum even when the collateral itself exceeds 1 TAO. Use `btcli explain
AmountTooSmall` for the error description.
Writes use the existing wallet, review, signing and MEV-shield submission flow.

The SDK also exposes block-pinned reads through `client.read`:
`lending_reserves`, `lending_position`, `lending_positions`,
`lending_open_quote` and `lending_close_quote`. Low-level `OpenLoan` and
`CloseLoan` intents accept exact amounts and explicit quote bounds for callers
that build their own transaction flow.

For an owner-aware opening or increase quote, supply both `coldkey_ss58` and
`hotkey_ss58` to `lending_open_quote`. Omitting both addresses keeps the fresh-loan
quote; it does not account for an existing position.
