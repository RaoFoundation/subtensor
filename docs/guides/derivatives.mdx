A **derivative position** lets you bet on the price of a subnet's alpha
without holding it. A **short** profits when alpha falls. A **long** profits
when alpha rises. You put down a **cushion** (a TAO deposit) and the chain
borrows a matching slice of the subnet pool for you. When you settle, the
borrowed slice goes back, the pool is paid its **interest**, and you get your
cushion plus or minus the price move.

You hold **one position per subnet**, and you move it with **one call**:
`add`. Add on the side you already hold and the position grows. Add on the
other side and that much comes off, paid out at today's price. Add more than
you hold and the position flips through zero. `close` settles all of it.

There are no synthetic tokens. Nothing is minted or burned. Every position is
built from the pool's own TAO and alpha. A position has **no expiry**. It
lives until you close it, or until its cushion can no longer pay its interest
and the chain hands it back to the pool. Nobody else can touch it.

The whole design is two numbers, and root sets both. The pool lends out at
most `pool_share` of itself per side (25%), and it charges `interest_rate` on
what it lends (25%), the same for shorts and longs. Everything else follows.

Shipped in the [v456 release](/releases/v456-upgrade). This guide uses the
same illustrative pool as the staking guides — **10,000 τ / 200,000 α at
0.05 τ/α** — with round numbers. Read live values with
[`derivatives-params`](/docs/query/derivatives-params) and
[`derivative-positions`](/docs/query/derivative-positions) before acting.

## What a position is

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **Cushion** | Your deposit, in TAO. It is held by the pallet while the position is open and paid back as the position settles, minus the interest and any loss. |
| **Leverage** | A multiple you choose on each add with `--leverage` (default 1). The runtime fixes a ceiling per side: 1x on shorts and 2x on longs. A position's own leverage is `exposure / cushion`, the blend of its adds. |
| **Exposure** | The TAO value the position moves with: leverage times your cushion. A 100 τ cushion at 1x is 100 τ of exposure; at 2x it is 200 τ. |
| **Tranche** | What one `add` contributes: its cushion, its slice, its interest. A position is the running sum of its tranches; nothing is tracked per tranche. |
| **Borrowed slice** | The fraction `phi` of the pool the position lifts out: `phi × TAO reserve` and `phi × alpha reserve`. |
| **Legs** | The three amounts the borrowed slice turns into once the opening trade is done: proceeds, debt, and escrow. The pallet tracks each in its own token. |
| **Proceeds** | What the opening trade produced. A short sells the lifted alpha, so its proceeds are TAO. A long buys alpha with the lifted TAO, so its proceeds are alpha. |
| **Debt** | What must go back to the pool at settlement: the lifted alpha (short) or the lifted TAO (long). |
| **Escrow** | The other half of the lifted slice. It sits untouched in the pallet and returns to the pool at settlement. |
| **Interest** | `interest_rate` (25%) of a tranche's TAO exposure per year, the same for both sides, fixed for the tranche when it is added and accrued per block. Once a week the chain takes it out of your cushion, buys alpha with it, and recycles the alpha: interest is buy pressure on the pool. Nothing is charged up front. |
| **Runway** | How long your cushion keeps paying interest at the current rate. When it cannot, the position is **starved**: the chain forfeits everything it holds for it to the pool, with no swap, and you get nothing. Adding cushion extends the runway. |
| **Equity** | What a close right now would pay you: cushion + proceeds − debt at the pool's quote − interest owed. An estimate; only you can act on it. |

One position per coldkey per subnet. Its side is simply the sign of what you
hold: a short that you add a bigger long to becomes a long.

## How add and settle work

The pallet never trades against you and never holds a book. It lifts a slice
of the pool, trades that slice through the same balancer swap you use when
you stake, and reverses the trade at settlement.

The deck below walks one position through all eight steps. Press play, or
step through with the arrows. Flip **side** to see a long instead of a short,
and **what happens next** to see the losing path as well as the winning one.

<DerivativesLifecycle />

Three details behind the slides:

- **Lifting is price-neutral.** The pallet removes the same fraction `phi`
  from both reserves ([`lift_liquidity`](/code/pallets/subtensor/src/staking/derivatives_pool.rs)).
  The price is a ratio, so it does not move. The pool is briefly shallower,
  which is why exposure is capped.
- **The open trade is a real swap.** A short pushes the price down at open; a
  long pushes it up. You pay slippage on the way in and on the way out,
  exactly like a spot trade of the same size. Because the pallet sells alpha
  it just lifted, rather than alpha it bought, there is no round trip that
  can be arbitraged.
- **Returning is price-neutral too.** At settlement the pool gets its slice
  back through
  [`return_liquidity`](/code/pallets/subtensor/src/staking/derivatives_pool.rs),
  which adds an uneven TAO/alpha pair without moving the price. Any profit
  you take leaves the pool a little lighter on one side; any loss you pay
  leaves it heavier. The interest does not travel this way: it buys alpha and
  recycles it, so it is the one thing a position does that always moves the
  price up.

The borrowed slice of each tranche is fixed when it is added:

$$
\phi = \frac{L \cdot \text{cushion}}{\text{TAO reserve}}
$$

where $L$ is the leverage you chose (`leverage_percent / 100`), at most the
side's ceiling: 1.0 for a short, 2.0 for a long. A 100 τ cushion on a
10,000 τ pool lifts 1% of the pool at 1x (100 τ and 2,000 α) and 2% at 2x
(200 τ and 4,000 α). See
[`pool_fraction`](/code/pallets/derivatives/src/position.rs).

The slice is bounded on both tokens: no more TAO than $L \times$ cushion, and
no more alpha than that TAO is worth at the subnet's **moving-average price**,
the same smoothed price emission uses. On an untouched pool the two bounds
agree. If someone moves the spot pool in the same block, one of them binds
instead, so a dump does not buy a bigger alpha slice and a pump does not buy
a bigger TAO one. The cap is checked the same way, against the smaller of the
live and the smoothed reserve.

### One position, many adds

Every quantity in a position is a plain sum: cushion, proceeds, debt, escrow,
exposure, and interest per year. That is what makes one call enough
([`do_add`](/code/pallets/derivatives/src/settle.rs)):

| You hold | You add | What happens |
|----------|---------|--------------|
| nothing | 100 τ short | A short opens with a 100 τ cushion. |
| 100 τ short | 50 τ short | A second slice is lifted and sold at today's price. Cushion 150 τ, every leg is the sum of two lifts, the interest is the sum of both. |
| 150 τ short | 50 τ long | One third of the short is settled at today's price: a third of the debt is bought back, a third of the escrow returned, and a third of the cushion, less interest and less or plus the price move, comes back to you. Nothing is deposited. |
| 100 τ short | 250 τ long at 1x | The whole short is settled and paid out. The 150 τ that remain open a long with a 150 τ cushion; only that 150 τ is taken from you. |
| 100 τ short | 100 τ long at 1x | The short is settled; you are flat. Anything left over that is below the minimum deposit (0.1 τ) is dropped rather than opened. |

A reduction is a fraction of the position, and it is the same fraction of
every leg ([`do_settle`](/code/pallets/derivatives/src/settle.rs)). Because
a position has one blended entry price, a fraction of it is underwater
exactly when the whole is: an underwater reduction pays you nothing for that
share and the pool takes everything the pallet held for it, same as a full
close. The rest stays open.

## What you get back

<DerivativesPayoff />

Three things to read off the picture:

- **The line crosses your cushion at the open price.** Close with no price
  move and you get your 100 τ back, minus the interest and a little slippage.
- **The slope is your leverage.** At 1x, alpha down 20% pays a short about
  20 τ on a 100 τ cushion. At 2x, alpha up 20% pays a long about 40 τ on the
  same cushion, and alpha down 20% costs it about 40 τ. The chart shows each
  side at its ceiling.
- **The line stops at zero.** If the closing trade cannot repay what the
  position borrowed, the position is underwater: you are paid nothing,
  whatever the pallet still holds goes to the pool, and the pool carries the
  remaining shortfall
  ([`do_settle`](/code/pallets/derivatives/src/settle.rs)). At leverage $L$
  that happens once the price moves against you by $1 / L$: a doubling for a
  1x short, a halving for a 2x long. The pool share cap keeps that gap small
  relative to the pool.

Why a 2x ceiling on longs and 1x on shorts? At 1x a long's cushion equals its
debt, so it can never be underwater and the pool never carries any of its
risk. That sounds safe, but it also means a 1x long does nothing a spot buy
does not do, and pays interest for it. At 2x a long is a real instrument, and its worst case
for the pool, a halving, is about as rare as the doubling that wipes a 1x
short. The two ceilings then carry comparable risk. They are runtime
constants (`MaxShortLeverage`, `MaxLongLeverage`), changed only by an
upgrade, and you may always pick less.

## Weekly interest

**Interest** is what the pool charges you for lending you its slice. It is
the only cost besides slippage, and it is one law for both sides.

Two things happen on two different clocks. The interest **accrues** every
block: the amount you owe grows a little each block. The chain **collects**
it once a week: it takes the amount owed out of your cushion. Nothing is
charged up front.

### The two parameters

Root sets two numbers with `sudo_set_params`. They are the whole design.

| Parameter | Default | Meaning |
|-----------|---------|---------|
| `pool_share` | 25% | The most that all open positions on one side of one subnet may borrow together, as a share of the reserve they borrow from. |
| `interest_rate` | 25% a year | The interest on a tranche's TAO exposure. The same for shorts and longs. |

Changing `interest_rate` later does not reprice a running position. Each
tranche keeps the rate it was added at, and the position carries the sum as
`interest_per_year` in TAO
([`interest_due`](/code/pallets/derivatives/src/position.rs)).

### Accrual: what you owe grows every block

A tranche's interest per year is fixed when you add it: `interest_rate` times
the tranche's TAO exposure. From that block on it accrues in proportion to
the blocks it has been open:

$$
\text{interest} = \text{interest\_rate} \times \text{exposure} \times \frac{\text{blocks open}}{\text{blocks per year}}
$$

A year is 2,628,000 blocks at 12 seconds a block. A 100 τ short at 1x has
100 τ of exposure, so it owes 25 τ a year: about 0.48 τ a week, or 0.068 τ a
day. A 100 τ long at 2x has 200 τ of exposure and owes twice that. Leverage
costs in proportion to what it borrows. Pool size and `pool_share` do not
enter. Add and close in the same block and you owe nothing.

### Collection: the chain takes it once a week

When a position opens, the chain gives it a **due block** one week ahead:
50,400 blocks, which is 7 days at 12 seconds a block. The position is listed
in a queue under that block.

When the chain reaches the due block, the pallet's `on_initialize` hook (code
that runs at the start of every block, before any transaction) collects the
position ([`collect_due`](/code/pallets/derivatives/src/settle.rs)). It takes
the interest accrued since the last collection out of the cushion, and it
books the position again one week later. You never pay by hand. The
position's trade, leverage, and exposure are not touched.

Each block runs at most 20 collections. If more positions than that fall on
one block, the rest are collected in the blocks that follow. If the chain
ever stalls, the queue catches up and charges the exact time elapsed.

Adding on your own side does **not** reset the weekly clock. The add brings
the running total up to date and the due block stays where it was. A
reduction does not move it either.

Closing or reducing pays the interest owed so far, through the same path, at
the moment you settle.

### Buy pressure: where the interest goes

The collected TAO does not sit in the pool as TAO. It is spent on the pool
itself: the pallet buys alpha with it at the going price, then **recycles**
the alpha. Recycling takes the alpha out of circulation and returns it to the
subnet's unissued supply, the same thing that happens to a registration fee.

The result is **buy pressure**. The pool keeps the TAO, the alpha leaves
circulation, and the price ticks up a little. Shorts and longs pay the same
way, so interest on either side is a buy of the subnet's alpha. The interest
is the one thing a position does that always moves the price up.

If the pool will not swap a dust amount, that TAO goes back to the pool as
plain TAO instead.

### When the cushion runs dry

The chain checks the cushion only at a weekly collection. If the cushion
cannot cover the interest owed at that block, the position is **starved**.
The chain forfeits it
([`forfeit`](/code/pallets/derivatives/src/settle.rs)):

1. Every TAO and every alpha the pallet holds for the position goes back to
   the pool as it is, with no swap. For a short that is the cushion, the
   proceeds, and the escrow, all TAO. For a long it is the cushion in TAO
   plus the proceeds and the escrow in alpha.
2. You are paid nothing.
3. The position is removed and `PositionClosed` is emitted with
   `closed_by: Starved`.

Because nothing is traded, a forfeit moves no price.

**Runway** is how long your cushion keeps paying at the current rate.
`btcli deriv list` shows it in days. At 25% a year, a fresh 1x position lasts
four years and a fresh 2x long two years. To extend the runway, add cushion on
your own side.

The rate is interest on the pool's liquidity, not an option premium. What
protects the pool from a position that turns dangerous is the cap and the
leverage ceilings. What the interest does is make time cost something: a
slice cannot be held out of the pool for free, so a position that is not
earning its keep is one its owner closes, and one whose owner walks away pays
the pool until its cushion is gone.

<Callout type="note">
`btcli deriv list` shows an **estimated** equity: cushion + proceeds − debt −
interest, with the debt priced on a constant-product curve. The chain's Balancer
quote differs a little from that, so treat `health` near the line as a
warning, not a verdict.
</Callout>

## Add to a position

Replace netuid `7` with your target subnet. `short` and `long` are the same
call with the side fixed. `--amount` is the TAO the tranche is sized by;
`--leverage` is the exposure as a multiple of it (default 1), up to the side's
ceiling. When the add is on your position's side (or you have none),
`--amount` is deposited as cushion. When it goes against your position,
nothing is deposited: it only sizes how much comes off.

```bash
# Check the two parameters and the fixed limits first
btcli deriv params --json

# Short subnet 7 with a 100 τ cushion at 1x (dry-run, then execute)
btcli deriv short --netuid 7 --amount 100 --dry-run -w my_coldkey
btcli deriv short --netuid 7 --amount 100 -w my_coldkey

# Add 50 τ more to the same short
btcli deriv short --netuid 7 --amount 50 -w my_coldkey

# Take 30 τ of exposure off it, paid out at today's price
btcli deriv long --netuid 7 --amount 30 -w my_coldkey

# Flip: settle the whole short and open a long with what is left of 300 τ at 2x
btcli deriv long --netuid 7 --amount 300 --leverage 2 -w my_coldkey
```

The same in Python:

```python
await client.execute(bt.AddPosition(netuid=7, side="Short", amount=100), wallet)
await client.execute(bt.AddPosition(netuid=7, side="Long", amount=300, leverage=2), wallet)
```

The call fails if:

| Error | Why |
|-------|-----|
| `SubnetNotDynamic` | The subnet has no pool (root, or a static subnet). |
| `DepositTooLow` | The deposit is below the minimum, 0.1 τ. |
| `LeverageOutOfRange` | `--leverage` is zero or above the side's ceiling. |
| `ZeroExposure` | The slice rounds to nothing against the pool, or the pool would swap it for nothing. |
| `PoolCapExceeded` | All open positions of that side already borrow `pool_share` (25%) of the lent reserve, or your slice alone would take the whole pool. A `pool_share` of zero means root has paused new positions. Try a smaller cushion or wait for others to close. |

```python
positions = await client.prices.derivative_positions(coldkey_ss58="5C...")
one = await client.prices.derivative_position(coldkey_ss58="5C...", netuid=7)

```

Settling is atomic: [`do_settle`](/code/pallets/derivatives/src/settle.rs)
reverses the open trade, repays the pool, collects the interest, pays you, and
returns the slice — or the whole call reverts. You may close, or reduce, at
any block.

## How a position closes

Only you can close your position. `close` settles the position of the
account that signs the call, and no other
([`close`](/code/pallets/derivatives/src/lib.rs)). There is no liquidator,
no expiry date, and no price that forces a close.

A position ends in one of three ways. The `PositionClosed` event names which
one in `closed_by`:

| Closer | What happened |
|--------|---------------|
| `Owner` | You called `close`, or you added the other side for at least your whole position. |
| `Starved` | A weekly interest collection found a cushion that could not pay. The chain forfeited the position to the pool. |
| `Dissolution` | The subnet was dissolved. The position was cash-settled at one fixed price. |

Two words for what follows. The **pot** is the TAO a settlement has to work
with: your cushion plus whatever the closing trade brings in. A position is
**underwater** when its pot cannot buy back, or repay, the whole debt.

### Closing a short

A short owes the pool alpha (its **debt**). It holds the TAO that alpha was
sold for (its **proceeds**). It also holds untouched TAO from the lifted
slice (its **escrow**). [`do_settle`](/code/pallets/derivatives/src/settle.rs)
runs these steps in one call. Either all of them land or none does.

1. Work out the interest owed up to this block.
2. Build the pot: cushion plus proceeds.
3. Buy back exactly the alpha debt from the pool, paying from the pot. The
   swap stops when the debt is covered, or when the pot is empty. If the pot
   runs out first, the alpha still missing is the **shortfall**.
4. Take the interest owed out of what is left in the pot.
5. If there is a shortfall, the position is underwater. The whole rest of the
   pot goes to the pool, and you are paid nothing.
6. The interest TAO buys alpha from the pool, and that alpha is recycled (see
   [Weekly interest](#weekly-interest)).
7. Whatever is left in the pot is paid to you, in TAO.
8. The pool gets back the alpha that was bought, the escrow TAO, and anything
   forfeited. This return does not move the price.

### Closing a long

A long owes the pool TAO (its **debt**). It holds the alpha that TAO bought
(its **proceeds**). It also holds untouched alpha from the lifted slice (its
**escrow**). The steps mirror the short:

1. Work out the interest owed up to this block.
2. Start the pot with your cushion.
3. Sell the proceeds alpha back to the pool. The TAO it brings goes into the
   pot.
4. Repay the TAO debt from the pot. If the pot cannot cover all of it, the
   unpaid TAO is the **shortfall**.
5. Take the interest owed out of what is left in the pot.
6. If there is a shortfall, the position is underwater. The whole rest of the
   pot goes to the pool, and you are paid nothing.
7. The interest TAO buys alpha from the pool, and that alpha is recycled.
8. Whatever is left in the pot is paid to you, in TAO.
9. The pool gets back the TAO that was repaid, the escrow alpha, and anything
   forfeited. This return does not move the price.

### Closing part of a position

Adding on the other side closes part of your position instead of all of it.
`add` compares the exposure you ask for (deposit times leverage) with the
exposure you hold:

- **Less than you hold.** That fraction of the position runs the steps above,
  with the same fraction of the cushion as its pot. The interest owed by the
  whole position is paid first from that pot, then from the cushion that
  stays. The rest of the position stays open, and `PositionReduced` is
  emitted. Nothing is deposited.
- **Your whole position, or more.** The whole position runs the steps above.
  If the part of your deposit past the flip point is at least 0.1 τ, it opens
  a new position on the other side. Only that part is taken from you.

## What never happens

**No liquidation.** Nobody but you can close your position. There is no
health check, no liquidator, and no third-party `close`. A position that
anyone could close once the price moved against it would invite a squeeze:
pump the price, close the shorts, and sell into the buybacks the closes
force. So there is no such door.

**No expiry.** A position has no term. Nothing forces a mark to market on a
date, nothing has to be rolled, and no add is ever refused for being late.

**No forced close on price.** A price move against you, on its own, never
ends your position. It costs you only if you close into it, or if you hold
until the interest has used up the cushion. Until then the pool carries that
exposure: it holds your cushion and the slice's TAO and alpha, and it keeps
collecting interest. What protects the pool is the `pool_share` cap and the
leverage ceilings, not a liquidator.

**Underwater but alive.** If the price has moved so far against you that a
close would pay nothing, you can still hold. If the price comes back, so does
your equity. If it does not, the cushion runs down and the position is
forfeited at a weekly collection in the end (see
[When the cushion runs dry](#when-the-cushion-runs-dry)). Either way the
pool is whole, and the most you lose is the cushion.

## Longs do not earn emission

Subnet emission is weighted by each subnet's moving price. A long lifts the
spot price for as long as it is open, so without a correction a team could
long its own subnet and be paid emission for it. The chain closes that door:
the price the emission EMA tracks is computed with every open long's alpha
counted back into the pool
([`get_emission_alpha_price`](/code/pallets/subtensor/src/staking/stake_utils.rs)).

A long removes alpha from the reserve and leaves the TAO where it was, so the
alpha the pool is missing is exactly the long side's `Footprint`. Adding it
back gives the price the pool would show if every long handed its slice back
in kind. On a constant-product pool that is the pre-long price to the rao.

Shorts are not corrected. A short lowers the price on purpose, and the
emission follows it. Spot price, swaps, and the price-neutral injection of
emission into the pool all still use the real reserves; only the emission
weight uses the adjusted price.

## What happens when the subnet is deregistered

**Deregistration**, or **dissolution**, is the removal of a subnet. The
chain cleans up the subnet's state over the blocks that follow, in phases.
Your position does not survive it. It is turned into TAO, by the chain, at
one price, and paid to you.

This is a **cash settlement**. Nothing is swapped through the pool. Nothing
is netted between one position and another. Each position is priced alone.

The steps, in order:

1. **Derivatives settle first.** The settlement of positions is the first
   cleanup phase
   ([`DerivativesSettle`](/code/pallets/subtensor/src/subnets/dissolution.rs)).
   It runs before stakes are converted and before any staker is paid, while
   the pool and the stake records still exist.
2. **One price is fixed.** Before the first position is settled, the chain
   reads the pool's **spot price** once and stores it
   ([`spot_price`](/code/pallets/subtensor/src/staking/derivatives_pool.rs)).
   It emits `DissolutionPriced` with that price as a `tao / alpha` pair.
   Every position on the subnet settles at this same price, whatever block it
   is reached in.
3. **A short's debt is converted, rounded up.** The alpha a short owes
   becomes a TAO amount at the fixed price. The rounding goes up, in the
   pool's favour. The short's pot is its cushion plus its proceeds.
4. **A long's alpha is credited, rounded down.** The alpha a long holds is
   handed to the pool, and its TAO value at the fixed price is added to the
   long's pot. The rounding goes down, in the pool's favour. The long's TAO
   debt is already TAO.
5. **The debt is repaid from the pot.** Then the interest owed up to this
   block is taken from what is left.
6. **Underwater pays nothing.** If the pot could not cover the debt, the rest
   of the pot goes to the pool and you are paid nothing.
7. **The remainder is paid to you in TAO.**
8. **Everything else goes back to the reserves.** The repaid debt, the
   interest, the escrow, and any alpha handed back all land in the pool's
   reserves. The stakers are paid from those reserves in the later phases.

Three guarantees around those steps
([`settle_at_dissolution`](/code/pallets/derivatives/src/settle.rs)):

- **It never blocks dissolution.** Each block settles as many positions as
  its weight budget allows, then the phase resumes in the next block until no
  position is left. A transfer that cannot go through is logged, the position
  is dropped from the book, and the cleanup moves on.
- **There is no cap on positions.** A subnet with more positions than one
  block can settle is settled over as many blocks as it takes.
- **Order does not matter.** Because the price is fixed once and nothing is
  swapped, the payout of a position does not depend on which positions were
  settled before it.

A small example, at a fixed price of 1 τ per α, with 1 τ of interest owed on
each position:

| Position | Owes | Holds | Pot | Paid to owner | Back to the pool |
|----------|------|-------|-----|---------------|------------------|
| Short A | 60 α | 160 τ (cushion + proceeds) | 160 τ | 160 − 60 − 1 = **99 τ** | 61 τ + its escrow TAO |
| Short B | 40 α | 140 τ (cushion + proceeds) | 140 τ | 140 − 40 − 1 = **99 τ** | 41 τ + its escrow TAO |
| Long C | 60 τ | 60 τ cushion + 60 α | 60 + 60 = 120 τ | 120 − 60 − 1 = **59 τ** | 61 τ, plus its 60 α and its escrow alpha |

The chain does **not** net the 100 α the shorts owe against the 60 α the
long holds and trade the 40 α difference against the pool. It settles the
three positions one at a time at the same price.

Because nothing is bought back, a short's own price impact is not reversed.
A short that sold its slice down the curve and then saw the subnet dissolve
is charged at the price the pool last showed, not at what climbing the curve
back would have cost. That is the one place a short is paid for its own
impact, and it is bounded by the cap on how much of a pool can be shorted.

## Parameters

Two numbers, root-set with `sudo_set_params`, read with
[`derivatives-params`](/docs/query/derivatives-params). Defaults from
[`DerivativesParams::defaults`](/code/pallets/derivatives/src/position.rs).

| Parameter | Default | Meaning |
|-----------|---------|---------|
| `pool_share` | `25%` | Largest share of the lent reserve all open positions of one side on one subnet may borrow together. Zero pauses new positions; open ones still reduce and close. |
| `interest_rate` | `25%` | Interest per year on a tranche's TAO exposure, both sides, accrued per block. Fixed for each tranche when it is added. |

Three limits are runtime constants, visible in the same read and changed only
by an upgrade:

| Constant | Value | Meaning |
|----------|-------|---------|
| `MaxShortLeverage` | `100` | Highest leverage a short may choose. 100 = 1x. |
| `MaxLongLeverage` | `200` | Highest leverage a long may choose. 200 = 2x. |
| `MinDeposit` | `0.1 τ` | Smallest cushion a tranche may add. Also the smallest surplus a flip will open. |

### Events

Every state change emits an event, so an indexer or a watcher can follow the
book without reading storage:

| Event | When |
|-------|------|
| `PositionAdded` | A tranche was added (or a position opened): owner, subnet, side, the deposit, leverage, legs, exposure added, and the position's exposure after it. |
| `PositionReduced` | Part of a position was settled: owner, subnet, side, the fraction unwound, the payout, interest paid, any shortfall, and the exposure left. |
| `PositionClosed` | A position settled in full: why (`Owner`, `Starved`, or `Dissolution`), the payout to the owner, interest paid, and any shortfall the pool carried. A starved position pays what was left of its cushion as interest and has no shortfall: nothing was swapped. |
| `ParamsSet` | Root set the two parameters. |
| `DissolutionPriced` | A dissolving subnet's positions are about to be cash-settled: `tao / alpha` is the spot price every one of them settles at. |

The storage behind them is public too: `Positions`, `Footprint` (borrowed per
side per subnet), `OpenByNetuid` (who holds a position on a subnet),
`Due` (the interest queue, positions by the block they are next collected)
and `NextDue` (the first slot not yet collected), `Params`, `PalletHotkey`,
and `DissolutionPrice` while a subnet's positions are being settled.

## Rules worth remembering

- One position per coldkey and subnet. Its side is the sign of what you hold;
  adding the other side takes from it or flips it.
- Cushions are TAO, and they come back as TAO.
- You can reduce or close at any block. Nobody else can. There is no expiry
  and no liquidation: a price move never takes your position, only an empty
  cushion does. Watch the runway and add cushion if you must.
- One interest rate for both sides, on exposure: 25% a year, accrued per
  block and collected from your cushion by the chain once a week, as a buy of
  the subnet's alpha. A 2x long pays twice a 1x short of the same cushion,
  because it borrows twice as much.
- Slippage is real on both legs. Size positions against the pool's depth,
  not against your balance.
- Profit comes from the pool; loss goes to the pool. The leverage ceilings
  (1x shorts, 2x longs) and the 25% cap bound how much either can move the
  pool. You pick your own leverage under the ceiling on every add; the
  position's leverage is the blend.
- A long moves the spot price but not the price emission is weighted by. A
  short moves both.
- If the subnet is deregistered, your position is cash-settled for you at one
  fixed spot price, with no swap, before any staker is paid. You get the
  remainder in TAO; an underwater position gets nothing.

For the pool mechanics underneath, see [Staking pools](/docs/concepts/staking-pools).
