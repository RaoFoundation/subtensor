A **derivative position** lets you bet on the price of a subnet's alpha
without holding it. A **short** profits when alpha falls. A **long** profits
when alpha rises. You put down a **cushion** (your deposit) and the chain
borrows a matching slice of the subnet pool for you. When you settle, the
borrowed slice goes back, the pool is paid its **rent**, and you get your
cushion plus or minus the price move.

You hold **one position per subnet**, and you move it with **one call**:
`add`. Add on the side you already hold and the position grows. Add on the
other side and that much comes off, paid out at today's price. Add more than
you hold and the position flips through zero. `close` settles all of it.

There are no synthetic tokens. Nothing is minted or burned. Every position is
built from the pool's own TAO and alpha. A position has **no expiry**. It
lives until you close it or until it can no longer pay one more day of its
rent; after that anyone may close it for you and is paid for doing so.

The whole design is two numbers. The pool rents out at most `max_pool_share`
of itself per side (10%), and it charges `rate_per_year` on what it lends
(20%), the same for shorts and longs. Root picks those two; everything else
follows.

Shipped in the [v456 release](/releases/v456-upgrade). This guide uses the
same illustrative pool as the staking guides — **10,000 τ / 200,000 α at
0.05 τ/α** — with round numbers. Read live values with
[`derivatives-params`](/docs/query/derivatives-params) and
[`derivative-positions`](/docs/query/derivative-positions) before acting.

## What a position is

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **Cushion** | Your deposit. TAO from your coldkey balance, or, where root has switched it on for the side, alpha already staked on one of your hotkeys. It is held by the pallet while the position is open and paid back in kind as the position settles, minus the rent and any loss. |
| **Leverage** | A multiple you choose on each add with `--leverage` (default 1). Root sets a ceiling per side: today 1x on shorts and 2x on longs. A position's own leverage is `exposure / cushion`, the blend of its adds. |
| **Exposure** | The TAO value the position moves with: leverage times your cushion. A 100 τ cushion at 1x is 100 τ of exposure; at 2x it is 200 τ. |
| **Tranche** | What one `add` contributes: its cushion, its slice, its rent. A position is the running sum of its tranches; nothing is tracked per tranche. |
| **Borrowed slice** | The fraction `phi` of the pool the position lifts out: `phi × TAO reserve` and `phi × alpha reserve`. |
| **Proceeds** | What the opening trade produced. A short sells the lifted alpha, so its proceeds are TAO. A long buys alpha with the lifted TAO, so its proceeds are alpha. |
| **Debt** | What must go back to the pool at settlement: the lifted alpha (short) or the lifted TAO (long). |
| **Escrow** | The other half of the lifted slice. It sits untouched in the pallet and returns to the pool at settlement. |
| **Rent** | One rate for both sides, `rate_per_year` (20%) of a tranche's TAO exposure per year, fixed for the tranche when it is added and paid to the pool whenever the position settles. The position carries it as a per-day amount. One day is booked the moment you add; after that the rent runs per block. |
| **Equity** | What a close right now would pay you: cushion + proceeds − debt at the pool's quote − rent owed. Negative means underwater. |
| **Health** | A position is healthy while its equity covers one more day of rent. Once it does not, anyone may close it and is paid the rent owed (at least one day of it); you get nothing. Adding cushion restores health. |

One position per coldkey per subnet. Its side is simply the sign of what you
hold: a short that you add a bigger long to becomes a long.

## How add and settle work

The pallet never trades against you and never holds a book. It lifts a slice
of the pool, trades that slice through the same balancer swap you use when
you stake, and reverses the trade at settlement.

The deck below walks one position through all eight steps. Press play, or
step through with the arrows. Flip **side** to see a long instead of a short,
and **what happens next** to see the losing path as well as the winning one.

<DerivativesLifecycle />

Three details behind the slides:

- **Lifting is price-neutral.** The pallet removes the same fraction `phi`
  from both reserves ([`lift_liquidity`](/code/pallets/subtensor/src/staking/derivatives_pool.rs)).
  The price is a ratio, so it does not move. The pool is briefly shallower,
  which is why exposure is capped.
- **The open trade is a real swap.** A short pushes the price down at open; a
  long pushes it up. You pay slippage on the way in and on the way out,
  exactly like a spot trade of the same size. Because the pallet sells alpha
  it just lifted, rather than alpha it bought, there is no round trip that
  can be arbitraged.
- **Returning is price-neutral too.** At settlement the pool gets back its slice
  plus the rent through
  [`return_liquidity`](/code/pallets/subtensor/src/staking/derivatives_pool.rs),
  which adds an uneven TAO/alpha pair without moving the price. Any profit
  you take leaves the pool a little lighter on one side; any loss you pay
  leaves it heavier.

The borrowed slice of each tranche is fixed when it is added:

$$
\phi = \frac{L \cdot \text{cushion}}{\text{TAO reserve}}
$$

where $L$ is the leverage you chose (`leverage_percent / 100`), at most the
side's ceiling: `max_short_leverage_percent / 100` (1.0 on mainnet) for a
short, `max_long_leverage_percent / 100` (2.0) for a long. A 100 τ cushion on
a 10,000 τ pool lifts 1% of the pool at 1x (100 τ and 2,000 α) and 2% at 2x
(200 τ and 4,000 α). See
[`pool_fraction`](/code/pallets/derivatives/src/position.rs).

### One position, many adds

Every quantity in a position is a plain sum: cushion, proceeds, debt, escrow,
exposure, and rent per day. That is what makes one call enough
([`do_add`](/code/pallets/derivatives/src/settle.rs)):

| You hold | You add | What happens |
|----------|---------|--------------|
| nothing | 100 τ short | A short opens. Cushion 100 τ, one day of rent booked. |
| 100 τ short | 50 τ short | A second slice is lifted and sold at today's price. Cushion 150 τ, every leg is the sum of two lifts, the rent is the sum of both. |
| 150 τ short | 50 τ long | One third of the short is settled at today's price: a third of the debt is bought back, a third of the escrow returned, and a third of the cushion, less rent and less or plus the price move, comes back to you. Nothing is deposited. |
| 100 τ short | 250 τ long at 1x | The whole short is settled and paid out. The 150 τ that remain open a long with a 150 τ cushion; only that 150 τ is taken from you. |
| 100 τ short | 100 τ long at 1x | The short is settled; you are flat. Anything left over that is below `min_deposit_tao` is dropped rather than opened. |

A reduction is a fraction of the position, and it is the same fraction of
every leg ([`do_settle`](/code/pallets/derivatives/src/settle.rs)). Because
a position has one blended entry price, a fraction of it is underwater
exactly when the whole is: an underwater reduction pays you nothing for that
share and the pool takes everything the pallet held for it, same as a full
close. The rest stays open.

## What you get back

<DerivativesPayoff />

Three things to read off the picture:

- **The line crosses your cushion at the open price.** Close with no price
  move and you get your 100 τ back, minus the rent and a little slippage.
- **The slope is your leverage.** At 1x, alpha down 20% pays a short about
  20 τ on a 100 τ cushion. At 2x, alpha up 20% pays a long about 40 τ on the
  same cushion, and alpha down 20% costs it about 40 τ. The chart shows each
  side at its current ceiling.
- **The line stops at zero.** If the closing trade cannot repay what the
  position borrowed, the position is underwater: you are paid nothing,
  whatever the pallet still holds goes to the pool, and the pool carries the
  remaining shortfall
  ([`do_settle`](/code/pallets/derivatives/src/settle.rs)). At leverage $L$
  that happens once the price moves against you by $1 / L$: a doubling for a
  1x short, a halving for a 2x long. The 10% pool cap keeps that gap small
  relative to the pool.

Why a 2x ceiling on longs and 1x on shorts? At 1x a long's cushion equals its
debt, so it can never be underwater and the pool never carries any of its
risk. That sounds safe, but it also means a 1x long does nothing a spot buy
does not do, for rent. At 2x a long is a real instrument, and its worst case
for the pool, a halving, is about as rare as the doubling that wipes a 1x
short. The two ceilings then carry comparable risk. Both are dials root can
turn, and you may always pick less than the ceiling.

Cushions are TAO by default. Root can switch alpha cushions on per side
(`alpha_cushion_shorts`, `alpha_cushion_longs`); they ship off, so a subnet
team cannot post alpha it minted to itself as collateral. An alpha cushion is
sized at the spot price when you add and valued at the pool's quote in
equity, so a falling price hurts a long with an alpha cushion twice.

The rent is the only cost besides slippage, and it is the same law for both
sides. Each tranche's rent is fixed when you add it, from the parameters in
force then; one day is booked at once and the rest accrues per block, charged
at every settlement:

$$
\text{rent per day} = \frac{\text{rate\_per\_year} \times \text{exposure}}{365}
$$

with `rate_per_year` 20% on mainnet. A 100 τ short at 1x has 100 τ of
exposure and pays about 0.055 τ a day: 1.6 τ a month, 20 τ a year. A 100 τ
long at 2x has 200 τ of exposure and pays twice that: leverage costs in
proportion to what it borrows. Pool size and pool share do not enter. The
rate is rent on the pool's liquidity, not an option premium; the pool's
protection against a position that turns out to be dangerous is the cap and
the leverage ceilings, not the price of the rent. What the rent does is make
time cost something: a position that is not earning its keep is one its owner
closes. Root can still set a different rate for one subnet with a per-subnet
override.

Changing the parameters later does not reprice a running position: the rent
per day is stored in the position when the tranche is added. See
[`fee_owed`](/code/pallets/derivatives/src/position.rs) and
[`DerivativesParams::fee_per_day`](/code/pallets/derivatives/src/position.rs).

<Callout type="note">
`btcli deriv list` shows an **estimated** equity: cushion + proceeds − debt −
rent, with the debt priced on a constant-product curve. The chain's Balancer
quote differs a little from that, so treat `health` near the line as a
warning, not a verdict.
</Callout>

## Add to a position

Replace netuid `7` with your target subnet. `short` and `long` are the same
call with the side fixed. `--amount` is the TAO the tranche is sized by;
`--leverage` is the exposure as a multiple of it (default 1), up to the side's
ceiling in `btcli deriv params`. When the add is on your position's side (or
you have none), `--amount` is deposited as cushion, in TAO, or in the subnet's
alpha with `--in alpha` from stake on `--hotkey` where root allows it. When it
goes against your position, nothing is deposited: it only sizes how much comes
off.

```bash
# Check the pallet's live parameters first, and whether subnet 7 is paused or re-capped
btcli deriv params --netuid 7 --json

# Short subnet 7 with a 100 τ cushion at 1x (dry-run, then execute)
btcli deriv short --netuid 7 --amount 100 --dry-run -w my_coldkey
btcli deriv short --netuid 7 --amount 100 -w my_coldkey

# Add 50 τ more to the same short
btcli deriv short --netuid 7 --amount 50 -w my_coldkey

# Take 30 τ of exposure off it, paid out at today's price
btcli deriv long --netuid 7 --amount 30 -w my_coldkey

# Flip: settle the whole short and open a long with what is left of 300 τ at 2x
btcli deriv long --netuid 7 --amount 300 --leverage 2 -w my_coldkey
```

The same in Python:

```python
await client.execute(bt.AddPosition(netuid=7, side="Short", amount=100), wallet)
await client.execute(bt.AddPosition(netuid=7, side="Long", amount=300, leverage=2), wallet)
```

The call fails if:

| Error | Why |
|-------|-----|
| `SideDisabled` | Root has switched that side off, chain-wide with `sudo_set_params` or on this subnet with `sudo_set_subnet_override`. Reducing a position is never blocked. |
| `SubnetNotDynamic` | The subnet has no pool (root, or a static subnet). |
| `DepositTooLow` | The tranche's cushion is worth less than `min_deposit_tao` (0.1 τ). |
| `AlphaCushionDisabled` | You passed `--in alpha` and root has not switched alpha cushions on for that side. |
| `LeverageOutOfRange` | `--leverage` is zero or above the side's ceiling (`max_short_leverage_percent` / `max_long_leverage_percent`). |
| `ExposureTooLarge` | `phi` would be 100% or more of the pool. |
| `PoolCapExceeded` | All open positions of that side already borrow `max_pool_share` (10%, or the subnet's override) of the lent reserve. Try a smaller cushion or wait for others to close. |

## Watch and close

```bash
# Your open positions, with rent so far, estimated equity, and health
btcli deriv list -w my_coldkey
btcli deriv list --coldkey <ss58> --netuid 7 --json

# Close your position on subnet 7
btcli deriv close --netuid 7 -w my_coldkey

# Positions on subnet 7 that anyone may close (unhealthy), lowest equity first
btcli deriv closable --netuid 7

# Close one of them and be paid for it
btcli deriv close --netuid 7 --owner <their-ss58> -w my_coldkey
```

```python
positions = await client.prices.derivative_positions(coldkey_ss58="5C...")
one = await client.prices.derivative_position(coldkey_ss58="5C...", netuid=7)
closable = await client.prices.derivative_positions_on_subnet(netuid=7)
await client.execute(bt.ClosePosition(netuid=7), wallet)
```

Settling is atomic: [`do_settle`](/code/pallets/derivatives/src/settle.rs)
reverses the open trade, repays the pool, collects the rent, pays you, and
returns the slice — or the whole call reverts. You may close, or reduce, at
any block.

### The rent ledger

The position keeps two rent numbers: `fee_per_day`, the summed rent of its
tranches, and `fee_accrued`, what is owed and not yet paid. Every add books
one day of the new tranche's rent into `fee_accrued` at once; from then on
the summed rent accrues per block. Every settlement, partial or full, pays
the whole `fee_accrued` to the pool out of the share being settled (and out
of the cushion that stays if that share cannot cover it), then the clock
starts again from zero. Adding and closing in the same block therefore still
pays one day of rent; holding a tranche for a day pays two.

## No expiry, one health check

A position ends in one of two ways: you close it, or it runs out of equity.
Only the second lets anyone else touch it
([`close`](/code/pallets/derivatives/src/lib.rs)).

There is no term. Nothing forces a mark to market on a date, nothing has to be
rolled, and no add is ever refused for being late. What keeps a slice of the
pool from being held out for good is the rent: every day open costs the same
share of exposure, so a position that is not earning its keep is one its
owner closes, and one that stops paying is closed for them.

**Health.** What bounds a position is its own equity: what a close at this
block would pay you, after the pool is repaid at its current quote and the
rent owed is taken out
([`Position::equity`](/code/pallets/derivatives/src/position.rs)).

- While equity covers **one more day of rent**, the position is **healthy**
  and only you can touch it.
- Once equity drops below one day of rent, from time or from price, it is
  **unhealthy**. Anyone may then `close` it. The closer is paid the rent owed
  plus whatever is left after the pool is repaid; if that is less than one day
  of rent, the pool tops it up to one day. You are paid nothing.
- Before that happens, you can **add cushion**: a new tranche's cushion is
  equity too, so adding on your own side restores health. Or reduce, or close.

At 20% a year, time alone takes a 1x position about five years to starve, so
health is mostly about price. The one-day buffer is what makes a liquidation
pay for itself: at the moment a position becomes closable it still holds
about a day of rent, so the closer's bounty comes out of the position, not
the pool. The pool only pays the floor when a price jump takes a position
from healthy to underwater between blocks.

The chain runs no sweep of its own. Closing what is unhealthy is a job for
anyone with a bot: read
[`derivative-positions-on-subnet`](/docs/query/derivative-positions-on-subnet),
filter on `healthy`, and call `close` on what you find. The SDK's `healthy` is
an estimate on a constant-product curve; the chain decides with its own
Balancer quote and rejects a close of a position it still finds healthy with
`OwnerOnly`.

## Longs do not earn emission

Subnet emission is weighted by each subnet's moving price. A long lifts the
spot price for as long as it is open, so without a correction a team could
long its own subnet and be paid emission for it. The chain closes that door:
the price the emission EMA tracks is computed with every open long's alpha
counted back into the pool
([`get_emission_alpha_price`](/code/pallets/subtensor/src/staking/stake_utils.rs)).

A long removes alpha from the reserve and leaves the TAO where it was, so the
alpha the pool is missing is exactly the long side's `Footprint`. Adding it
back gives the price the pool would show if every long handed its slice back
in kind. On a constant-product pool that is the pre-long price to the rao.

Shorts are not corrected. A short lowers the price on purpose, and the
emission follows it. Spot price, swaps, and the price-neutral injection of
emission into the pool all still use the real reserves; only the emission
weight uses the adjusted price.

## Subnet dissolution

If a subnet is dissolved while positions are open, settling them is the
**first** cleanup phase
([`DerivativesSettle`](/code/pallets/subtensor/src/subnets/dissolution.rs)),
before any staker is paid. Dissolution is a forced close of every position on
the subnet at that block's price, and all of them close together as **one
atomic swap**.

The chain sums the alpha every short owes (`AlphaToSettle` for the short
side) and the alpha every long holds (the long side). The two cancel; only
the difference is quoted against the pool, exactly as the pool's own swap
would price it
([`dissolution_price`](/code/pallets/subtensor/src/staking/derivatives_pool.rs)).
The TAO and alpha of that one net swap are stored once, before the first
position is settled, and reported in `DissolutionPriced`; their ratio is the
**dissolution price**, and every position settles at it. No position moves
the price it or any other gets, and a short and a long that offset each other
pay no slippage at all. With one lone position open, the result is what
`close` would have paid in that block, to the rounding of a quote.

Each position is then settled at that price with no further swap
([`settle_at_dissolution`](/code/pallets/derivatives/src/settle.rs)). A
short's alpha debt is charged in TAO at the price; a long's alpha is handed
to the pool and credited in TAO at the price. The rent owed is paid. What is
left of the cushion and proceeds is yours; if it is negative the position is
underwater, you are paid nothing, and the pool takes what the pallet holds,
as at any settlement. A short that is in the money at that block is paid its
gain, first, in TAO. The stakers are paid from what is left.

## Parameters

Root-set, read with [`derivatives-params`](/docs/query/derivatives-params).
Mainnet defaults from
[`DerivativesParams::defaults`](/code/pallets/derivatives/src/position.rs).
Root sets them with `sudo_set_params`, which rejects a zero leverage ceiling,
`max_pool_share`, or `rate_per_year`; use the enable flags to pause a side.
Every value is a dial: changing one applies to tranches added from then on. A
running position keeps the rent per day it has; its next add is checked
against the new ceilings and cap.

| Parameter | Default | Meaning |
|-----------|---------|---------|
| `shorts_enabled` | `true` | Short tranches may be added. Existing positions can always be reduced or closed. |
| `longs_enabled` | `true` | Long tranches may be added. |
| `max_short_leverage_percent` | `100` | Highest leverage a short may choose. 100 = 1x. |
| `max_long_leverage_percent` | `200` | Highest leverage a long may choose. 200 = 2x. |
| `alpha_cushion_shorts` | `false` | Whether a short tranche may deposit alpha as its cushion. TAO is always accepted. |
| `alpha_cushion_longs` | `false` | Whether a long tranche may deposit alpha as its cushion. |
| `max_pool_share` | `10%` | Largest share of the lent reserve all open positions of one side on one subnet may borrow together. |
| `rate_per_year` | `20%` | Rent per year on a tranche's TAO exposure, both sides, carried per day (`/ 365`). Fixed when added; one day booked at once, then per block. |
| `min_deposit_tao` | `0.1 τ` | Smallest cushion a tranche may add, in TAO value. Also the smallest surplus a flip will open. |

### Per-subnet overrides

Root can also pause a side, change the cap, or change the rent on one
subnet without touching the rest, with
`sudo_set_subnet_override(netuid, override)`. Read it with
[`derivatives-subnet-override`](/docs/query/derivatives-subnet-override) or
`btcli deriv params --netuid N`. Passing `None` removes the override and puts
the subnet back on the global parameters.

| Field | Meaning |
|-------|---------|
| `shorts_enabled` | Replaces the global switch for adding short tranches on this subnet. |
| `longs_enabled` | Replaces the global switch for adding long tranches on this subnet. |
| `max_pool_share` | When set, replaces the global cap on this subnet. Must be non-zero. |
| `rate_per_year` | When set, replaces the global rent for tranches added on this subnet. Must be non-zero. The lever for a small pool whose pump risk the flat rate underprices. |

An override affects adds only. Positions already open on a paused subnet can
be reduced and closed as usual; they cannot grow while the side is paused.
This is the lever to reach for when one pool misbehaves: pause the side, let
the open positions run off or close, and lift the pause when the pool is
healthy again.

### Events

Every state change emits an event, so an indexer or a watcher can follow the
book without reading storage:

| Event | When |
|-------|------|
| `PositionAdded` | A tranche was added (or a position opened): owner, subnet, side, the tranche's deposit (`Tao` or `Alpha`), leverage, legs, exposure and rent per day, plus the position's exposure after it. |
| `PositionReduced` | Part of a position was settled: owner, subnet, side, the fraction unwound, TAO and alpha returned, rent paid, any shortfall, and the exposure left. |
| `PositionClosed` | A position settled in full: who closed it (`Owner`, `Liquidator(account)`, or `Dissolution`), TAO and alpha returned to the owner, rent paid, any shortfall the pool carried, and the `bounty` paid to a liquidator. |
| `ParamsSet` | Root replaced the global parameters. |
| `SubnetOverrideSet` | Root set or removed a subnet's override. |
| `DissolutionPriced` | A dissolving subnet's positions are about to settle as one net swap: the alpha shorts owe, the alpha longs hold, and the `tao` and `alpha` of the netted swap; `tao / alpha` is the price every one of them settles at. |

The storage behind them is public too: `Positions`, `Footprint` (borrowed per
side per subnet), `AlphaToSettle` (alpha owed by shorts and held by longs, per
side per subnet), `OpenByNetuid` (who holds a position on a subnet), `Params`,
`SubnetOverrides`, `PalletHotkey`, and `DissolutionPrice` while a subnet's
positions are being settled.

## Rules worth remembering

- One position per coldkey and subnet. Its side is the sign of what you hold;
  adding the other side takes from it or flips it.
- Cushions are TAO unless root has switched alpha on for the side. A cushion
  holds both, tracked separately, and comes back in kind.
- You can reduce or close at any block. There is no expiry. Keep equity above
  one day of rent (add cushion if you must) or anyone may close you out and
  keep that rent.
- One rent for both sides, on exposure: 20% a year, accrued per block. A 2x
  long pays twice a 1x short of the same cushion, because it borrows twice as
  much.
- Slippage is real on both legs. Size positions against the pool's depth,
  not against your balance.
- Profit comes from the pool; loss goes to the pool. The leverage ceilings
  (1x shorts, 2x longs) and the 10% cap bound how much either can move the
  pool. You pick your own leverage under the ceiling on every add; the
  position's leverage is the blend.
- A long moves the spot price but not the price emission is weighted by. A
  short moves both.

For the pool mechanics underneath, see [Staking pools](/docs/concepts/staking-pools).
